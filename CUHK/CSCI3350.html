Quantum Mechanics

Schrödinger equation states that $$ i \hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} | \psi(t) \rangle = \hat H | \psi(t) \rangle $$

We don't actually care about quantum mechanics, so the only thing important is that this is an unitary action on the state vector.

Linear Algebra

Refers to the MATH2040 notes for the basics. There are some notations that are unique here, though. For example, $0$-based are used instead of $1$-based. Also the followings:

Hilbert Space is a Complete Inner-Product Space. The completeness is in the metric sense.

Dirac Notation is essentially instead of using arrows, we use $| \psi \rangle$ and $\langle \psi |$ to denote column and row vectors respectively, with $\langle \psi | = | \psi \rangle^{\dagger}$ and $A^\dagger$ being the conjugate transpose of $A$. This means the inner product of $|\phi\rangle$ and $|\psi\rangle$ can be conveniently written as $\langle \phi | \psi \rangle$. Projection Operator $\Pi_{\psi}$ along $|\psi\rangle$ can also be written conveniently as $| \psi \rangle \langle \psi | $. We also abbreviate $| k \rangle = e_k$ and $| \pm \rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (| 0 \rangle \pm | 1 \rangle)$. The basis $\{ | 0 \rangle, | 1 \rangle \}$ is called the computational basis, whereas $\{ | + \rangle, | - \rangle \}$ is called the Hadamard basis.

Some unitary matrices that are often used are Pauli Matrices $X$ (bit-flip), $Y$ (combination), $Z$ (phase-flip) and Hadamard Matrix $H$: $$ X = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \quad Y = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -i \\ i & 0 \end{bmatrix} \quad Z = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \quad H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} $$

The spectral decomposition of Hermitian (observable) $A$ can be stated as $A = \sum_{m} \lambda_m \Pi_m = \sum_{m} \lambda_m | \psi_m \rangle \langle \psi_m |$.

Consider a bipartite Hilbert Space $\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B$. For $| \psi \rangle \in \mathcal{H}$, if $| \psi \rangle = | \psi_A \rangle \otimes | \psi_B \rangle $ for some $| \psi_A \rangle \in \mathcal{H}_A$ and $| \psi_B \rangle \in \mathcal{H}_B$, then $| \psi \rangle$ is called separable, and entangled if otherwise. We can define analogously for unitary operators as well.

The Four Postulates

The four postulates are stated below:

  1. A quantum physical system is described by a state vector, which is a unit vector $ | \psi \rangle $ in some Hilbert Space.
  2. The evolution of closed quantum system is described by a unitary action $U$, that is $ | \psi' \rangle = U | \psi \rangle$.
  3. The measurement of quantum system can be described by a collection of measurement operators $\{ M_m \}$ satisfying the completeness condition $ \sum_{m} M_m^{\dagger} M_m = I $, where the outcome is $m$ and the state vector collapses to $ \widehat{M_m | \psi \rangle} $ with probability $|| M_m | \psi \rangle ||^2$.
  4. The state space of a composite quantum system is the tensor product of the state spaces of the component physical systems.
