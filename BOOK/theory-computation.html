Introduction to the Theory of Computation

Can a computer think? What can computer solve? What kind of problems can a computer solve efficiently and practically? All these questions are philosophical. But "computer" is a vague wordings - is a vending machine a computer? Or an electronic calculator? Or some sort of teletype printer? Or even abacus? As you can see - we need some sort of clear definition of "computer" and "problem" to reason about them.

Following Aristotle, we use computational models, a model that supports a certain set of atomic operations, as an abstraction of computers.

  • Part One goes through weaker automata, such as the Deterministic Finite Automaton (DFA), Non-deterministic Finite Automaton (NFA) and the Pushdown Automaton (PDA).
  • In Part Two and Three, we focus on the strongest known model to human kind that can be implemented - the Turing Machine (TM).
    • Part Two focuses on Computability Theory, which asks the question - What class of problems can be solved by Turing Machine?
    • Part Three focuses on Complexity Theory, which asks a similar question - What class of problems can be solved efficiently, measured in time or space, by Turing Machine?

All these questions can be somewhat answered via precise and elegant definitions and deductions.
